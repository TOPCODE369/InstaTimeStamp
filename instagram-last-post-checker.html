How to Check When Someone Last Posted on Instagram

Whether you're a brand vetting an influencer, a fan checking if a creator is still active, or just curious about when a post was actually made — Instagram makes it surprisingly hard to find the exact date and time. Here's everything you need to know, including the fastest method that works on mobile.

Instagram intentionally hides exact post times. On mobile — where over 90% of people use the app — all you get is a vague relative time like "3 days ago" or "2 weeks ago". The exact date and time is buried, and for the most recent post of a specific account, there's no built-in way to find it quickly.

But there are four ways to get the information you need. We'll start with the fastest.

Method 2 — Hover on Desktop to See the Date

2
Hover over the timestamp on desktop
Desktop only Exact date

If you are on a laptop or desktop computer, Instagram shows you the exact date when you hover your mouse over the relative timestamp on a post.

  • 1Open Instagram in your browser on a computer
  • 2Navigate to the post you want to check
  • 3Find where it says "3 days ago" or "2 weeks ago" below the post
  • 4Hover your mouse cursor over that text and hold it there
  • 5A small tooltip appears showing the exact date — e.g. "September 19, 2026"

⚠️ Limitation: This method only shows the date, not the exact time. And it only works on desktop — there's no hover on mobile. For the precise time down to the second, use Method 1.

Method 3 — Check the Profile Grid Manually

3
Look at the profile grid for relative time
Mobile ✓ Desktop ✓

The simplest approach — just open the account's profile and look at the most recent post. Instagram will show a relative time like "3 hours ago", "yesterday", or "5 days ago" directly on the post.

  • 1Go to the Instagram profile you want to check
  • 2Look at the first post in their grid — this is always their most recent
  • 3Tap it to open — you'll see a relative time stamp below the post

⚠️ Limitation: This only gives you a rough idea — "3 days ago" tells you nothing about the exact time, and "2 weeks ago" could mean anywhere from 8 to 14 days. For accuracy, use Method 1.

Method 4 — Use Google to Find Recent Posts

4
Search Google for recent Instagram posts
Mobile ✓ Desktop ✓

Google indexes public Instagram posts and often shows when they were published in search results. This works best for public accounts with high engagement.

  • 1Open Google
  • 2Search for: site:instagram.com/username — replacing "username" with the actual account handle
  • 3Google search results often show a date next to each post link
  • 4Click on the most recent result to see the post

⚠️ Limitation: Google doesn't index every Instagram post, and its timestamps can be days behind. This method is useful as a quick check but not reliable for accuracy.

Why Does Instagram Hide Exact Post Times?

Instagram's decision to show relative times — "2 hours ago", "5 days ago" — rather than exact timestamps is a deliberate design choice. There are a few reasons behind it.

First, relative times are cleaner and simpler on mobile screens where space is tight. "3 days ago" takes less space and is arguably easier to process quickly than "Sep 17, 2026 at 11:42 PM".

Second, hiding exact times subtly reduces the anxiety around posting at the "perfect" time. If users could see that a post got 10,000 likes in the first two hours, it might create more pressure around timing.

Third, Instagram wants to reduce the ability to precisely reverse-engineer their algorithm. If you could see the exact post time and the exact engagement over time, you'd have a very clear picture of how the algorithm amplifies certain posts over others.

That said, the timestamp is not truly hidden — it's encoded inside every post's URL and shortcode. Tools like InstaTimestamp simply decode that information and present it in a readable format.

Can You Check the Last Post Date Without Following Someone?

Yes — for public accounts. All four methods above work on any public Instagram account without needing to follow them or have an Instagram account yourself.

For private accounts, only followers can see their posts. If you do not follow a private account, their posts are not visible to you and their timestamps cannot be checked by any method.
